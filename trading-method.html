THE METHOD

Six checks. In this order.

The first check matters most. If price is nowhere near a Perfect Entry 1 zone, I leave the chart alone.

01 / LOCATION

Start at the zone

Open the 15-minute chart. Is price near a Perfect Entry 1 zone? If no, stop here.

02 / DIRECTION

Check the wider trend

On the weekly chart, check the last 2–3 candles for a rough direction. On the daily chart, look for a consistent direction across about 12 candles. Sideways or unclear? Stop.

03 / FILTER

Check the TEMA ribbon

Back on M15, check the 45-minute TEMA ribbon. Above it: buys only. Below it: sells only. Match the daily direction and avoid chasing price far from the ribbon.

04 / PATTERN

Wait for break and retest

Let price break the relevant level, come back to test it, and show continuation. Switch to M5 when you need a closer look at the retest.

05 / PLAN

Choose stop and target

Stop just beyond the far side of the TEMA cloud, the M15 Perfect Entry 1 band, or nearby rejection wicks when those define the setup. The usual target is the centre of the next Perfect Entry 1 level. Look for roughly 3:1 potential reward to risk or more.

06 / MANAGEMENT

Take the exit that fits

At around halfway, I may take a satisfactory profit or move the stop to entry. Prop firm consistency rules, the size of a position, a distant zone, and the end of day can justify an earlier exit.

Before placing an order: check major scheduled news, at least on Forex Factory. Many of my best placements have been around 12:00–15:00 UK time, often shortly before 14:00. This is an observation, not a guarantee.

ACCOUNT NOTES

Same chart. Different risk.

Prop firm trading

I use defined stops and position sizing that follows the firm's rules. Some examples were closed well before their drawn targets because of consistency limits. A good entry can still end in a loss.

Small account flips

These are speculative accounts funded only with money I can afford to lose entirely. I have sometimes used heavy margin and traded without a stop, watching a 3-period RSI at 10 and 90 for context. I have seen around 30% drawdowns. An extreme RSI does not promise a reversal.

On a small account I may add positions until free margin remaining is around 50% of balance. Above roughly $200–$300 I use less; on $400, for example, I might leave about $270 as free margin. Margin used and money at risk are different things.

Leveraged forex and CFDs can cause rapid losses. These examples are educational records of my decisions, not instructions to copy my sizing or a claim of typical outcomes. Read the FCA's CFD risk information.
