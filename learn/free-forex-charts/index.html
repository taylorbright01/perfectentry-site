Free forex charts: build a useful workspace before adding more indicators

Your chart should make the next decision easier. If every new indicator sends you in a different direction, the problem may be the process around the chart. Here is a practical way to build that process with Perfect View, starting with the free workspace.

By Taylor Bright · Founder, Perfect EntryPublished 5 November 20265 min read
The short answer

A useful free forex chart needs readable candles, clear timeframes, drawing tools and reliable information about its data. Start with one market, one context timeframe and one entry timeframe. Add indicators only when they answer a specific question.

First, check what the chart is showing

Before drawing a level, check the symbol, timeframe and most recent candle time. A forex chart is a view of a particular data source. Differences in broker prices, candle boundaries and available history can change the shape of a candle or an indicator reading. A chart that looks familiar is not automatically the same feed as your execution account.

Perfect View displays a data status. If it says sample data, you are looking at an illustration rather than current market prices. If the feed is delayed, a previous candle can remain visible. Check freshness before using a chart to inform a decision, and confirm executable prices with your broker.

Use the same source and candle convention throughout a test. Otherwise, you can end up comparing a rule on one chart with an outcome recorded on another.

Give each timeframe a job

Choose a context timeframe to describe the wider price structure and a second timeframe to inspect a setup. For example, a trader researching an intraday method might review the one-hour chart for context and the five-minute chart for detail. That is a workflow example, not a claim that those settings have an edge.

Write down what would count as a trend, range or invalid setup before switching between them. Without a fixed definition, it is easy to keep changing timeframes until one supports a trade you already want to take. Your checklist should explain when the answer is “wait”.

In Perfect View, choose your market and use the timeframe controls to move between views. Begin with enough history to understand the nearby structure, rather than treating the final candle as the whole story.

Add one indicator for one question

A moving average can summarise recent price direction. RSI can describe momentum. Neither removes the need to define an entry, an exit and a risk limit. If two studies answer essentially the same question, adding both may give you more colour without giving you much more information.

Perfect View includes standard studies such as RSI and moving averages without a paid membership. Start there. Our RSI and multiple-timeframe guide explains why a reading above 70 is not, by itself, a reason to sell.

Keep settings consistent while you test. Record the study name, period, timeframe and whether you used closed candles. Changing the settings after every losing trade makes it difficult to learn whether the original idea was useful.

Put the plan on the chart

Mark the price area you want to review, the point where your idea would be invalid and a potential exit. Perfect View provides drawing tools, Fibonacci levels and position planning tools so that the analysis can live alongside the candles.

Those marks describe a plan. They do not place a broker order or guarantee an execution price. A planned stop can be exceeded by gaps or slippage. For risk planning, check the instrument’s contract size, account currency and broker specifications rather than assuming all symbols have the same pip value.

A simple workspace checklist
  • Correct market, timeframe and data status.
  • A written condition for considering a setup.
  • A visible invalidation level and an exit rule.
  • A limit on risk across all open positions.
  • A saved example to review afterwards.

When does a paid indicator earn its place?

Free charting is enough to start learning and testing standard ideas. A paid indicator should add a specific capability to your process: perhaps a clearer way to display your setup, a defined signal method or settings that suit a workflow you already understand. Judge it by that job, the available evidence and how consistently you can use it.

Your first session in Perfect View

  1. Open the workspace and verify the data status.
  2. Choose one market and a context timeframe.
  3. Add one standard study and mark the nearby levels.
  4. Write the conditions that would make you act or stand aside.
