How to evaluate forex signals: currency strength, evidence and the role of CurrencyAI

A buy arrow answers one small question: what direction does this system suggest? Before you use it, you need to know why the candidate qualified, what invalidates it and how the wider record was produced. That is where signal selection and evidence become more useful than a screenshot.

By Taylor Bright · Founder, Perfect EntryPublished 5 November 20265 min read
The short answer

Evaluate a forex signal system by its entry and exit rules, complete timestamped record, costs, realised outcomes and drawdown. Currency context can help frame a candidate, but strength scores are model-dependent. CurrencyAI filters candidates using recent currency-side outcomes; approval does not guarantee a winning trade.

A forex signal expresses a view on two currencies

In EURUSD, EUR is the base currency and USD is the quote currency. Buying EURUSD expresses a relative view in favour of EUR against USD; selling expresses the opposite. CME Group’s FX quote convention guide explains that relationship.

This matters when several pairs appear to offer different trades. Buying EURUSD and GBPUSD creates a shared short-USD component. Buying USDJPY expresses a long-USD component. Different chart symbols do not automatically mean independent exposure.

The exact portfolio risk also depends on position size, stop distance, contract specifications and how prices move together. A currency label is a useful first check, not a substitute for measuring total risk.

What does a currency strength score mean?

A strength score is the output of a calculation. One tool might use percentage price changes across a basket; another might normalise momentum by volatility. Different baskets, lookbacks, weights and update times can produce different rankings without either tool having a universal claim to the “true” strength of a currency.

Ask what the score measures, which pairs contribute and whether the latest value uses a completed observation. Price-based strength and a score based on recent strategy outcomes are different concepts. Neither is the same thing as a forecast of central bank policy or the economic health of a country.

A hypothetical strong-EUR, weak-USD reading could support investigating a EURUSD buy candidate under a particular rule. It does not establish an entry price, a stop, an expected payoff or the probability of a win. Those require separate definitions and evidence.

Where CurrencyAI fits into the process

CurrencyAI is Perfect Entry’s rule-based filtering tool. As described on the product explanation page, it checks rolling currency-side outcomes when assessing incoming trade candidates. Candidates that pass its rules reach the approved signal feed; others are held back.

The sequence is candidate, context check, then approved feed or rejection. Recent behaviour of the tracked system informs selection. This is different from assuming that the tool reads every piece of economic news or independently predicts the whole currency market.

The useful question is whether the selection improves the characteristics you care about over a properly defined record. Fewer signals alone do not demonstrate improvement. An approved signal can lose, and a rejected candidate can go on to win.

A checklist for evaluating any signal record

Questions to ask before relying on a signal system
CheckWhat you need to understand
CommitmentWhat was recorded before the outcome was known?
CoverageAre losses, cancellations and open trades accounted for?
RulesHow are entry, stop, exit and forced closure defined?
CostsWhich spread, commission and slippage assumptions apply?
ReturnsWhat are the realised payoff distribution and drawdown?
ChangesWhich rule version generated each part of the record?

Use expectancy rather than win rate alone to connect the frequency of wins with their size. Ask whether the reported return comes from one exceptional event or from a broader set of observations. A visible loss is not a flaw to hide; it is part of understanding what the method does.

Compare approved signals with the wider candidate stream

To investigate a filter, compare clearly defined groups over the same period and with compatible outcome rules. The approved subset and wider stream may have different trade counts and exposures. Total R alone can favour the group that simply trades more, while average R alone can hide a very small sample.

Perfect Entry provides realised CurrencyAI results, a filter comparison and a signal ledger. Read the labels and coverage on each page. The comparison is a research resource, not proof that a filter caused improvement or that the same relationship will continue.

Be careful with hindsight: a rule chosen after inspecting the entire candidate history needs a later evaluation period. Our backtesting guide explains why a record used to tune a rule cannot also be treated as untouched evidence.

Use the signal as part of a trading process

Before considering a candidate, check the instrument, timeframe, timestamp and current status. Decide how much total risk you can accept and whether you already have related currency exposure. Compare the planned entry with the price actually available to you.

A tracked result does not mean that your account would receive the same fill. Broker spreads, slippage, availability and your own exit decisions can all change the outcome. CurrencyAI does not remove your responsibility for execution or risk management.

Frequently asked questions

Is CurrencyAI a guarantee of profitable forex signals?

No. It is a rule-based candidate filter. Approved signals can lose, and tracked outcomes can differ from your own execution.

Is currency strength the same as CurrencyAI’s outcome context?

No. Generic strength tools may measure price changes or momentum. CurrencyAI’s documented selection uses recent currency-side outcomes from the tracked signal process.
