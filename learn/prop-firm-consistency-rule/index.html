Prop firm consistency rules: calculate the profit you still need

A profitable account is not automatically eligible for a payout. A consistency limit, qualifying-day requirement, withdrawal cap or retained buffer may still matter. Start with the arithmetic, then check the actual rules for your account and payout cycle.

By Taylor Bright · Founder, Perfect EntryPublished 5 November 20265 min read
The short answer

For a best-day rule based on total net profit, divide your largest profitable day by your total net profit. If the limit is c, required total profit is at least best day ÷ c. Additional profit is that total minus current profit, assuming no later day becomes the new best day.

Check which consistency rule you actually have

“Consistency” is not one universal prop firm rule. The definition can depend on the provider, programme, account stage and payout option. A rule based on total net profit is different from one based only on the sum of profitable days. Qualifying-day requirements can be separate again.

For example, Topstep’s Express Funded Account documentation distinguishes between payout paths. That is a reason to read your own current terms, not to apply another provider’s percentage to your account.

The worked example below uses a hypothetical 20% best-day limit on total net profit, with equality allowed. It is not a statement of any named firm’s current offer. Confirm whether your boundary is “at or below” or “strictly below” before using a calculator.

The best-day formula

Let B be your largest profitable day in the relevant cycle, P your total net profit for that cycle, and c the allowed fraction. A 20% limit means c = 0.20.

Best-day share = B ÷ P × 100%
Required total profit = B ÷ c

This ratio is meaningful when P is positive. Use profit, not the full account balance. If your initial account size is $10,000 and you have earned $1,200, the denominator is $1,200 under this definition, not $11,200.

Include losses and costs where the rule defines net profit that way. If your firm uses a different denominator, period reset or daily accounting convention, the formula and inputs need to match that definition.

Worked example: $600 best day, 20% limit

Suppose your largest winning day is $600 and your current total net profit is $1,200. Your best day represents 50% of your profit: $600 ÷ $1,200 × 100.

At a 20% maximum, the required total is $600 ÷ 0.20 = $3,000. The remaining amount is $3,000 − $1,200 = $1,800. This assumes your best day remains $600 and no other condition adds a higher requirement.

Three additional $600 days would reach that total without increasing the largest day. That is an arithmetic illustration, not a trading target or a forecast. Trying to force those outcomes can introduce risk that the calculation does not justify.

Why a bigger winning day can move the target

If a later day earns $900, your best day becomes $900. At the same 20% limit, required total profit becomes $4,500. If that $900 day takes your current profit from $1,200 to $2,100, the remaining amount is now $2,400.

A loss can move you further away too: it reduces total net profit without reducing the recorded best day. Do not assume that “another $1,800” remains correct after the account changes. Recalculate using the current cycle’s complete record.

If the rule is strictly below 20%, exactly $3,000 against a $600 best day is not sufficient. You need more than the boundary, taking account of the firm’s calculation precision. The Perfect Entry calculator lets you choose an inclusive or strict boundary.

Keep profit, withdrawals and take-home pay separate

Suppose $3,000 is the available gross withdrawal under your actual rules and your trader split is 80%. The simple split gives $2,400 before any further deductions, fees or taxes. But reaching a $3,000 profit threshold does not necessarily mean the full $3,000 is withdrawable.

A required retained buffer, a percentage withdrawal cap or a fixed payout cap may reduce the gross amount first. A withdrawal may also change the distance to a loss limit. Review that remaining room before treating the payout as the end of the calculation.

Frequently asked questions

What is a 20% consistency rule?

Under the definition used here, your best profitable day must be no more than 20% of total net profit for the relevant cycle. Your provider may define it differently.

Do losing days affect consistency?

With total net profit as the denominator, a losing day reduces the total and increases the best-day percentage, assuming the best day is unchanged.

Does meeting the percentage guarantee a payout?

No. Other conditions, approval requirements, withdrawal caps and account-specific rules may still apply.
